My notes on Demian: Sinclair's youth

These are my notes on Demian, written as I read it. They are not a review. Some parts of this book I loved, and some parts I think are nonsense, and both are in here.

What this book is about

The author has written about his childhood but using different names. As a 10 year old, he describes his world view as a dichotomy. One side of it was his family, which represented the good: a happy family, religious, godly, the shining-with-brightness types. The other side was the outside, evil world. Neighbourhood uncles beating their wives, people in poverty, thieves. He writes about how this other world, so different, was still so close.

He recalls a major incident of his life, when he meets a first-rate brat from the neighbourhood, and another guy. For no reason he just seemed to follow what they were doing. One day they start bragging about all the bad things they've been doing. Our author had none. So he made up a story about stealing apples from a mill. This brat (Kromer, I think was his name) uses this leverage and blackmails the author to pay him money, or else he'd report this to the mill guy. The author was scared, so he followed. He lived and dreamt of fear, of the evil winning over him.

The extortion with Kromer continued until a new guy enrolled in school. This guy, Demian, was a mentalist personality. He liked reading people's thoughts and found pleasure in observing others.

Demian was also the first guy to give him a new view of the evil world. While discussing the story of Cain and Abel, he makes a few points. The stories written in such old books might be true, but misinterpreted by common folk. It's very much possible that Cain was just fearless and better than Abel and the cowards. When the cowards were asked why they aren't confronting Cain, rather than saying "we can't, we are cowards", they said that Cain has a mark given by god and blah blah.

Boarding school and Beatrice

Our dude is sent to boarding school. There he starts drinking and doing no-lifer stuff: broke, in debt to his friends. Still a virgin.

He saw a lady whom he named Beatrice, because she had boyishness in her and reminded him of the Dante and Beatrice painting. Even without talking to her, the thoughts about her changed him into a good guy. He started learning to paint, so that he could paint a more accurate portrait of her.

This part has nothing of importance to me. I consider this part hallucinations and bullshit thinking.

The ideas

"Everyone has a function, and that's discovering ourselves." Like we are part of a giant experiment where our task is to discover ourselves. He also says something about a man should seek his destiny, so, be vagabondish?

Another total bs take: that a student gathering was a herd gathering because they feared the unknown within them. I hate to see people like Demian talk so unreasonably.

But this one stuck with me: "Wanting someone to complete us is actually a part of ourselves we haven't yet claimed." That is one of the conclusions that come from the proposition that we only love the projection of another that we have in our mind.

It gets weird

After this, the book turns weird. Remember the painting of Beatrice? Well, turns out it's of Demian. And later turns out that it was not Demian, but Demian's mom, Eva.

Now our mc really thinks it's his fate to love Eva. And he's in love. Well, because he's the mc.

He longed to meet Demian's mom. And one day they did meet, at Demian's house (he lived with his mom). Eva knew all about our mc. After a few meets she even knew about mc's love.

Then comes the most interesting set of ideas in the book: when Eva explains love to Sinclair. I won't say exactly what she said, but here are my conclusions, what I made out of the whole thing.

  1. No two people have the exact same worldview. We see the world differently from each other. Throughout childhood, the unique experiences we've had build a filter through which we experience the world.
  2. When you love someone, you are loving an image of them that your mind has made for them. This image soothes something inside you, and this feel-good is what I'd call "love". (Sure, you could say love is more mature than this. Then make this a mature feel-good.)
  3. From 2, we can say that this image is a projection of ourselves.
  4. Love begins one sided, but needs fulfillment for more feel-good.
  5. Fulfillment is achieved at union. Union is when both people feel good about the projections being made on each other.
  6. Faith in love is one's confidence in their capability of love reaching fulfillment. Delusion is more attached to the result, i.e. the fulfillment.
  7. People should have faith in their love.
  8. Being too demanding, or being too hesitant, is unattractive. Confidence attracts. Law of balance.
  9. If the other person refuses to project, it's their choice. Fulfillment is a union, and union needs two.

Page 141, yeah, this book is weird bro.

Then war happens and the book ends.
